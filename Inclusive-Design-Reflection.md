Reading Ryder Carroll’s first-person account of ADHD and Bullet Journaling helped me understand how a simple organisational process supported him when information felt overwhelming. His experience reminded me that productivity tools should adapt to people’s needs, and that one person’s experience does not represent everyone.

At Focus Bear, I will use clear language, provide instructions in small steps and allow time for responses. I will ask users and teammates about their preferences rather than assume that everyone with ADHD or Autism needs the same support. For colleagues, I will provide written meeting summaries, clarify priorities and respect different working styles.

During QA testing, I will flag crowded screens, unexpected sounds, unclear navigation and messages that could make users feel guilty. One improvement I would propose is an optional view showing only the next small task, then seek feedback from neurodivergent users before recommending wider adoption.

A supportive response to a struggling user would be:

“Thanks for letting us know. It sounds frustrating to feel stuck. You don’t need to finish everything now. Would it help to choose one small step, such as opening the document? We can work from there at your pace.”
